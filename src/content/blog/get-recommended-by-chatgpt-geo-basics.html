What GEO is

GEO stands for generative engine optimization. The term comes from a 2023 research paper (KDD 2024) whose authors report it "can boost visibility by up to 40%" in AI answers in their tests. In plain words: SEO aims for a spot in a list of links. GEO aims for your name in the answer itself.

How AI assistants find businesses

An assistant answers from its training data or from a live web search. All four big ones can search:

  • ChatGPT "may search the web automatically when your question would benefit from current information" and uses your approximate location for local results (OpenAI).
  • Gemini can ground answers in the Google Search index (Google), and in Google Maps data.
  • Claude searches when a question benefits from current information, and cites its sources (Anthropic).
  • Grok can "search the web in real-time and browse web pages" (xAI).

So what counts is what a search finds about you now, and whether the assistant can read it.

What we see when we measure it

In October Foreground asked the four assistants the same three questions, in Dutch with web search on, for ten trades in Amsterdam, Utrecht and Haarlem:

  • Across 30 city pages they named 909 businesses. 508 were named in exactly one answer. Only 58 were named in six or more (source).
  • For eight trades, they named 723 businesses between them. Only 41 were named by all four (source).
  • For dentists and physios, chat answers named 186 businesses and Gemini working from Google Maps named 54. 149 of the 186 never came up in the Maps answers (source).

Each assistant has its own shortlist. Your Maps listing and your website are separate ways in.

The basics

1. Say who, what, where and price on your own site

In plain text, not in an image: your name, what you do, for whom, where, and what it costs. "Quality you can trust" gives an AI nothing to repeat. "Emergency plumber in Haarlem, same-day visits" does. Foreground found a riverside restaurant whose site never said where it is (source).

2. Use the same name, address and category everywhere

Google wants your Business Profile name to match the one "used consistently on your storefront, website, stationery", with a category as specific as possible (Google). Use exactly the same details on review sites and directories.

3. Add structured data

Structured data is a small JSON-LD block that states your facts for machines: schema.org LocalBusiness for a physical business, Organization otherwise. Google uses it "to understand the content of the page" (Google); its LocalBusiness guide requires only name and address. But Google says there's "no special schema.org structured data" needed for its AI Overviews or AI Mode (Google). Useful, but not a shortcut.

4. Answer the questions customers ask

Write pages or FAQs for the questions you hear on the phone: prices, areas, hours, who it's for. Specific questions favour small businesses: in Foreground's test of 11 customer niches, at least 3 of the top 5 businesses named were independents in 10 of them (source).

5. Get mentioned on the sites AI reads

In Foreground's Dutch niches, the main sources were the winners' own websites plus comparison sites such as funda.nl and trustoo.nl. One small hotel had been named in 0 of 47 answers. Then ChatGPT named it twice, both times citing the same review page on a Thailand travel blog (source). Find the directories, review sites and "best of" lists for your trade and city. Get on them. Google's local ranking also counts links to your site and your reviews (Google).

6. Let AI search crawlers in

Check your robots.txt. Search and training use separate crawlers:

  • OpenAI: "Sites that are opted out of OAI-SearchBot will not be shown in ChatGPT search answers." GPTBot is the training crawler, set separately (OpenAI). Your host or CDN must also allow OpenAI's search-bot IP addresses.
  • Anthropic: blocking Claude-SearchBot or Claude-User may reduce your visibility in Claude's search results; ClaudeBot is for training (Anthropic).
  • Google: Google-Extended covers Gemini training and grounding, and "does not impact a site's inclusion in Google Search" (Google).

With OpenAI and Anthropic you can refuse training and still be found. I found no official crawler list for Grok.

7. Measure it, more than once

Answers change between runs. Foreground asked the same questions twice, a few days apart: for 64% of businesses the count moved by at most 1 of 12, for 90% by at most 2 (source). So ask what a stranger would ask, without your business name, in two or more assistants, several times. Count how often you're named and who's named instead.

None of this guarantees a mention; OpenAI says placement in ChatGPT search "is not guaranteed". The basics remove the reasons an assistant can't name you. If counting by hand gets tedious, the free scan on foreground.info does it for one customer niche: 12 answers across four assistants.
