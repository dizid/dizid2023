"Once it's built, what does it cost me every month?" is one of the first questions people ask about a new web app, and one of the hardest to get a straight answer to. Below are the real numbers: the price lists of the services most small apps use, checked in November 2026, and my own bills for the apps I run myself. All prices are in US dollars unless they say euros.

Short answer: a small web app with a few hundred users can run for $0 to about $30 a month. Hosting is usually free. The bill comes from three places: the database, email, and AI usage if your app uses AI. A domain adds roughly $10 to $15 a year, and a payment provider takes a cut of each sale instead of a monthly fee.

Hosting: usually $0

Static sites and small apps fit in the free tiers of hosts like Netlify and Vercel. Netlify's current free plan gives 300 credits a month. A production deploy costs 15 credits, a GB of bandwidth 20 credits and 10,000 web requests 2 credits, so on a new free account the number of deploys can matter more than your traffic. The Personal plan is $9 a month for 1,000 credits.

My bill: $0. All my sites run on Netlify's free plan; my account is older and still on its earlier limits (100 GB bandwidth, 300 build minutes a month).

Database: from $0 to a few dollars per app

Serverless databases bill for the time they are awake, so a quiet app costs very little.

  • Neon (pricing): the free plan gives 100 compute hours and 1 GB of storage per project. The paid Launch plan charges $0.106 per compute hour and $0.35 per GB per month, with no monthly minimum. A database sleeps after 5 minutes without queries.
  • Supabase (pricing): the free plan gives a 500 MB database and 50,000 monthly active users, with 2 active projects; free projects are paused after a week without activity. Pro starts at $25 a month and includes $10 of compute credit.

My bill: $19.65 for October, for 17 database projects on Neon's Launch plan. The busiest was a travel site at about $4.70; my main SaaS cost about $3.70 and a job board about $3.10. Five idle projects cost close to nothing. The surprise: a good part of the travel site's database time came from search engine and AI crawlers waking it up, not only from people.

Email: free until you send a lot

Sign-up confirmations, password resets and receipts need a sending service. Resend's free plan covers 3,000 emails a month, at most 100 a day, from up to 3 domains. Pro is $20 a month for 50,000 emails. The 100-a-day cap is what usually pushes a growing app onto the paid plan: one newsletter or daily digest uses it up.

My bill: $20 a month, because one of my apps sends a daily job digest and several apps share the account.

AI: the line that can surprise you

If your app calls an AI model, you pay per use, and that is the one cost that grows with every user. A real example: one scan in my AI-visibility tool, which asks four AI assistants a set of customer questions, costs about $0.67 in API fees. A customer on a plan with eight scans a month can cost up to about $9 a month in AI alone, which is why every plan has a scan limit.

To estimate your own: count the words your app sends to the model and gets back for one typical action, multiply by the provider's price per token, then by the actions per user per month. Small models cost a fraction of large ones; test whether the small one is good enough before you default to the biggest.

Two rules keep this under control. Set a hard monthly spend limit in the AI provider's dashboard, not just an email alert. And cap usage in your own code: per user, per day, and per visitor for anything public. A public form that calls an AI model without limits is an open tap on your bill.

Payments: no monthly fee, a cut per sale

Stripe charges no setup or monthly fee. For standard cards from the European Economic Area it takes 1.5% + €0.25 per payment; UK cards 2.5% + €0.25; other international cards 3.15% + €0.25, plus 2% if a currency conversion is needed. On a €9 monthly subscription paid with a Dutch card that is about €0.39.

Domain and the small stuff

  • Domain: roughly $10 to $15 a year for a .com; some newer extensions are cheap the first year and much more on renewal, so check the renewal price before you buy.
  • Analytics: Cloudflare Web Analytics is free and doesn't use cookies.
  • Error tracking and uptime checks: free tiers are enough for a small app.

What it adds up to

  • Side project or early test, few users: $0 a month plus the domain. Everything fits in free tiers.
  • Small business app with real users and emails: about $5 to $40 a month: a paid database once the free one is too small or too sleepy, and an email plan once you send more than 100 a day.
  • App that uses AI on every action: the AI line usually becomes the largest one. Estimate it per user before you set your prices.

For all my own live apps together, databases and email come to about $40 a month. AI fees come on top and move with usage: one provider was $15 in October.

The hidden costs

  • Bots keep databases awake. Crawlers visit day and night. If every page view queries the database, a site nobody uses can still run up compute hours. Pages that don't change can be prerendered so they need no database at all.
  • Things that renew for projects you stopped. Domains and subscriptions auto-renew. Once a year, list every paid service and ask which app still uses it.
  • Paying "just in case". Always-on databases and bigger plans bought before there are users. Start on free or usage-based plans and upgrade when a limit is actually close.
  • Junk sign-ups. Bots that create accounts use up free email quotas and monthly-active-user limits. See how to stop fake sign-ups.
  • Your own time. Updates, a provider that changes its pricing, an expired key. Budget an hour or two a month for upkeep, even when nothing breaks.

Which services can stop spending and which only send a warning differs per vendor: see how to avoid a surprise bill.
